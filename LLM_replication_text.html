Introduction

In the last decades, Researchers in corpus linguistics gained access to increasingly massive corpora of natural language in different languages. This poses great opportunities, but also necessitates manual cleaning and processing of large amounts of data (Morin and Larsson 2025: 1). The rise of large language models (LLMs) is therefore a rather interesting development for corpus linguistic research, making it possible to handle larger datasets and corpora automatically. In recent years, many papers testing LLMs for assisting in linguistic research have been published (sources).

I aim to replicate a study by Morin and Larsson (2025), which presents a replicable method for leveraging LLMs for assistance in linguistic grammatical annotation. While the study was conducted with the model Claude Sonnet 3.5, I will attempt to replicate it using the same data, but with an open LLM (Mistral) in R.

This project is intended to embody the principles of Open Science in two respects: First, I want to contribute to the use and examination of open LLMs for analysis of linguistic data. While closed LLMs (like ChatGPT) are widely used in day-to-day life, their usage brings difficulties regarding data rights, privacy, etc. (see presentation for citation) among others. Many open alternatives are on the rise, but are not as widely used and investigated in studies as necessary (source?). Second, I want to conduct a replication and thereby participate in the central aim of making linguistic research more cumulative and robust.

Methodological approach

In planning this replication, I used the handbook by Röseler et al. (2025a) as a guide for the research process. On a general note, the following steps are listed (and conducted in this replication study):

  • choosing a target study,

  • deciding between different types of replications,

  • planning and running the new study,

  • analyzing the results,

  • discussing outcomes in the light of potential differences,

  • and publishing a report (Röseler et al. 2025b).
