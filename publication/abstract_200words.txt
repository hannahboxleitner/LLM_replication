Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being leveraged for corpus
annotation. In an early evaluation of such an application, Linguistics Vanguard published “a replicable method for automating grammatical annotation” (Morin & Marttinen Larsson 2025). Using Claude 3.5 Sonnet, the study reported an accuracy of 93%, claiming that the pipeline may be also effective for other linguistic tasks and languages other than English. We present a close replication, using the same data, but with two open-weight LLMs. Our results suggest that, as of 2026, commercial LLMs are not necessary for this type of task. In a second extended replication, we tested the need for the extensive training process outlined in Morin & Marttinen Larsson (2025). We compared it to a simpler few-shot approach, concluding that the procedure need not be as elaborate as in the original study. We use these replications as a starting point for a broader discussion of how linguists approach the use of LLMs in corpus annotation, arguing that issues around accessibility, replicability, transparency, and ethical concerns can and should inform the choice of our annotation tools. We warn against the risk of overgeneralising the results of a single classification task in English to other tasks and languages.
